How to Track Job Applications

Past the first dozen applications, memory stops working. You forget which company you applied to twice, who promised to get back to you "next week", and which recruiter you still owe a reply. A tracker fixes that, but only if it stays small enough that you actually update it.

This guide is the system I use: six columns, two views, and three rules about following up.

The six columns you need

ColumnWhy it matters
CompanySo you never apply to the same place twice by accident.
RoleLarge companies may have several openings you apply to separately.
StatusExactly one of Wishlist, Applied, Assessment, Interviewing, Offer, Rejected.
Next ActionThe one concrete thing you do next, such as "Email the recruiter".
Next Action DateWhen you do it. A follow-up without a date usually never happens.
Days since appliedShows at a glance which applications have gone quiet.

Resist adding more columns at the start. Salary, contact names and notes are useful later, but every extra field is one more reason to skip updating the tracker.

Two views: a board and a calendar

  • Board by status. One column per stage, so you can see how many applications are waiting and drag one to Interviewing when you get the call.
  • Calendar by Next Action Date. Each morning, open today's date and do what's there. That's the whole daily routine.

Three follow-up rules

  1. Every open application has a dated next step

    "Waiting to hear back" is not a next step. "Follow up on Thursday" is.

  2. Two follow-ups, then close it

    If two follow-ups get no reply, mark it Rejected and move on. No more "I'll give it another week".

  3. Once a week, check three numbers

    New applications this week, companies at the interview stage, and applications that haven't moved in 7 or more days. Each stale one gets a decision: one more follow-up, or close it.

FAQ

Should I use a spreadsheet or Notion? Either works if you keep the same columns. A spreadsheet is fine for a short search. Notion helps once you want a board view by status and a calendar of follow-up dates without building them yourself.

How many applications should I track? All of them, including the ones you think are long shots. A tracker only answers "who do I follow up with today" if every application is in it.

When should I give up on an application? After two follow-ups with no reply, mark it Rejected or Withdrawn and stop thinking about it. That keeps your active list short and honest.
